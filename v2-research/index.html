Both came out of my computer engineering degree, both are machine learning, and on both I built the models, worked out the analytical rationale, and wrote most of the paper.

I am second author on each. That understates the writing and overstates nothing else, which is why I would rather say plainly what I did than let the author order speak for me.

PAPER 01 Springer · peer reviewed · 2024

Brain Tumor Classification Using Deep Learning Techniques

Mahajan, P., Gupta, S., Bhanushali, D.K. & Joshi, D. (2024)
Lecture Notes in Networks and Systems, vol. 818, pp. 15–34. Springer, Singapore
Presented at the International Conference on Data Science and Applications, ICDSA 2023
doi:10.1007/978-981-99-7862-5_2

I built the models, developed the analytical rationale and wrote the majority of the paper.

The question

Classifying a brain tumour as benign, malignant or pituitary is a decision currently made with a biopsy, which is invasive, slow and carries its own risk. The question was how close deep learning on MRI has actually got, and whether the gap between published approaches is as large as it looks.

What we found

Traditional CNN approaches reported between 85.6% and 96.7% accuracy. Newer experimental architectures reported between 91.4% and 96.4%.

The ceiling is roughly the same. The newer approaches raise the floor rather than the best case, which is a more useful finding than a league table of headline numbers, and it is easy to miss if you only read the abstracts.

Being straight about what this is

It is a review and comparison, not original architecture work. We did not train a new model and beat a benchmark. What we did was read the field carefully, put the numbers on a common scale, and say what the comparison actually supports.

PAPER 02 IJRASET · peer reviewed · 2023

Body Pose Estimation using Deep Learning

Mahajan, P., Gupta, S. & Bhanushali, D.K. (2023)
International Journal for Research in Applied Science and Engineering Technology, 11(3), pp. 1419–1424
doi:10.22214/ijraset.2023.49688

I built the models, developed the analytical rationale and wrote the majority of the paper.

The constraint

Most pose estimation that works well relies on a depth camera or wearable sensors. Both are fine in a lab and useless in the places the technique would actually help, like a physiotherapy clinic or someone's front room.

So the system had to run on an ordinary webcam, in real time, on hardware a person already owns.

How it works

Built on MediaPipe. Skeletal landmarks are extracted frame by frame, joint angles are derived from those landmarks, and the resulting posture is classified. It runs on live video and recorded footage alike.

Where it struggles: partial occlusion, unusual camera angles and low frame rates. We said so in the paper rather than reporting only the conditions that flattered it.

What it is for

Physiotherapy and rehabilitation feedback, sports and form analysis, gesture interfaces, and fall detection. The common thread is that dropping the depth camera is what makes any of them affordable.
